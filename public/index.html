VPlan your visit

Know before you go.

Hours and rules are set by the venues and can change at short notice, especially around religious ceremonies. Here’s what applies this autumn.

I

Colosseum, Forum & Palatine

Hours
Colosseum from 8:30; Forum & Palatine from 9:00. Until 24 Oct: closes 18:30, last entry 17:30. 25 Oct–28 Feb: closes 16:30, last entry 15:30.
Closures
Closed 25 December and 1 January. Free on the first Sunday of every month and on 4 November: first-come, first-served and very crowded.
Good to know
  • Named tickets: bring a matching photo ID.
  • 75 min inside on standard tickets, 90 on Full Experience.
  • Small bags only; there’s no cloakroom in the park.
Insider tip
Book the 8:30 opening slot or a late-afternoon slot to avoid the heat and the tour-group peak.

II

Vatican Museums & Sistine Chapel

Hours
Mon–Sat 8:00–20:00, last entry 18:00; halls close 30 minutes earlier. Last Sunday of the month: 9:00–14:00, free entry, last entry 12:30.
Closures
Closed on Sundays, except the last Sunday of the month. Next holiday closures: 8, 25 & 26 Dec 2026, then 1 & 6 Jan 2027.
Good to know
  • Cover shoulders and knees; no hats.
  • No photos or phone use in the Sistine Chapel.
  • Tickets are non-refundable and valid only on the booked date.
Insider tip
Tuesday and Thursday tend to be calmest. Take the 8:00 slot to reach the Sistine before the late-morning crush.

III

St. Peter’s Basilica & Dome

Hours
Basilica: daily 7:00–20:00, last entry 19:15. Dome: 7:00–18:00 until 25 Oct, then 7:30–17:00 from 26 Oct.
Closures
No fixed closing day, but access can close at short notice for papal ceremonies. Wednesday mornings are usually disrupted by the General Audience.
Good to know
  • Entry is free; security applies to everyone.
  • Cover shoulders and knees; no hats inside.
  • Dome: 320 steps after the lift, not wheelchair accessible.
Insider tip
Arrive at the 7:00 opening, or after about 17:00, for the shortest security line. The Pietà is just inside on the right.

For the Vatican Museums and St. Peter’s Basilica, cover your shoulders and knees. Sleeveless or low-cut tops, shorts above the knee, miniskirts and hats aren’t allowed, and staff do turn people away. The Colosseum has no dress code, but wear shoes with good grip for the ancient paving. A light scarf in your bag solves most dress-code problems.

Yes. Colosseum tickets carry each visitor’s name, and you must show a matching photo ID at the entrance, so enter every name exactly as it appears on the passport. Names can be changed only once, up to 7 days before the visit, and only in limited cases. Reduced Vatican tickets need proof of eligibility, such as a student card. Carry your passport or national ID on every visit.

Most experiences can be cancelled for free up to 24 hours before they start, with a full refund. A few limited-capacity experiences, such as Colosseum Underground and Vatican early entry, need 72 hours’ notice. The exact policy is shown on every product before you pay. Time slots are fixed by the venues, so late arrivals and no-shows can’t be refunded.

Each experience lists exactly what you get: the official entry ticket where one is needed, a reserved time slot, and a guide or audio guide, plus headsets on group tours. Food, transfers and tips aren’t included. Skip-the-line means you skip the ticket-office queue with a reserved entry. Every visitor still goes through airport-style security, so allow time for it. Entry to St. Peter’s Basilica is free; with us you pay for guiding, reserved times and dome access.

Sometimes, if you’re on a guided tour. The Vatican lets guided groups use a direct passage from the Sistine Chapel to the Basilica when it’s open, but it’s closed on Wednesday mornings and during celebrations, so it’s never guaranteed. Independent visitors leave the Museums and walk 10–15 minutes around the Vatican walls to St. Peter’s Square, then go through security. If you plan to continue, don’t leave bags in the Museums’ cloakroom.

Mostly, with some limits. The Colosseum has step-free access on the ground level and lifts to the upper levels, but the Forum and Palatine have uneven ancient paving that is only partly accessible. The Vatican Museums are largely accessible by lift, and visitors with a certified disability of 67% or more, plus a companion, enter free with tickets issued at the entrance. The main floor of St. Peter’s is accessible, but the dome is not: even with the lift there are 320 steps. Tell us your needs before you book a guided tour and we’ll help you choose.

Official entry to the Colosseum is free for under-18s, though they still need a timed reservation; EU citizens aged 18–24 pay €2. At the Vatican Museums, children under 7 go free and minors and students pay the reduced rate. St. Peter’s Basilica is free for everyone. Guided tours have their own child rates, shown at checkout.

Your tickets arrive by email as soon as you book, ready to show on your phone, so there’s nothing to print. Your voucher gives the exact meeting point with a map pin and photo, plus how to recognise your guide. Arrive 15 minutes early for the Colosseum and St. Peter’s, and 30 minutes early for Vatican Museum tours, because security can be slow.
